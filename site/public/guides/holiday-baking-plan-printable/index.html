Holiday baking plan: how to schedule Christmas cookies, pies and treats without a last minute rush

Short answer: List each recipe with its batch size, a bake date and who it is for (gifts, a party or home). Bake doughs and treats that freeze well in late November and early December, keep the last few days for anything best fresh, and tick each batch off when it is done.

Columns to use

One row per batch:

  • Recipe
  • Batch size
  • Bake date
  • For: gift, party or home
  • Done (check box)

Bake in the right order

Many cookie doughs, shortbread and some cakes can be made ahead and frozen. Schedule those first. Save delicate decorated cookies, cream pies and anything that goes stale fast for the final two or three days before you need them. Always follow each recipe's own storage advice.

Match bake dates to events

Put party dates, school events and gift handoffs on the calendar first, then set each bake date a day or two before the event it is for. That way nothing is baked too early or too late.

Shop once

When the plan is done, add up the ingredients across all recipes and buy them in one trip. Flour, sugar, butter and eggs are the ones that usually run out mid baking day.

FAQ

How far ahead can I bake Christmas cookies?

It depends on the recipe. Many doughs and plain cookies freeze well for several weeks, while decorated or cream-filled treats are best made close to the day. Check each recipe's storage notes.

How many kinds of cookies should I make?

Pick a number you can bake comfortably on the days you have free. Three to five well made kinds is plenty for most cookie tins.
