Why a premium card needs a tracker

Cards like the Amex Platinum pack in many statement credits with different reset schedules, and several only work after you enroll or link a specific account. The amounts and the list change from time to time, so copy the current credits from your card's benefits page into the sheet rather than relying on a list online.

Columns

One row per credit:

  • Credit name
  • Value per period ($)
  • Cadence: Monthly, Quarterly, Semiannual or Annual
  • Needs enrollment? and Enrolled? (checkboxes)
  • Used this period ($)
  • Left = Value minus Used
  • Next reset date
  • Days to reset = Next reset minus TODAY()
  • How to trigger it (which merchant or app)

Reset date formulas

  • Monthly: =EOMONTH(TODAY(),0)+1
  • Quarterly: =EOMONTH(DATE(YEAR(TODAY()),CEILING(MONTH(TODAY())/3,1)*3,1),0)+1
  • Semiannual (Jan and Jul): =IF(MONTH(TODAY())<=6,DATE(YEAR(TODAY()),7,1),DATE(YEAR(TODAY())+1,1,1))
  • Calendar year: =DATE(YEAR(TODAY())+1,1,1)

Alerts that actually help

Add two conditional formatting rules. Red for money about to expire: =AND($F2>0,$H2-TODAY()<=14). Amber for credits you have not enrolled in yet: =AND($D2=TRUE,$E2=FALSE). Unenrolled credits are the easiest money to lose.

Does the card earn its fee?

Annual value of each credit = Value x periods per year. Total the amount you actually used this cardmember year with SUMIFS and divide by the annual fee. Count only credits you would have spent money on anyway, which gives an honest answer before the next fee posts.
