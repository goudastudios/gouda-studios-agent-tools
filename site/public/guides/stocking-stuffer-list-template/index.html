Stocking stuffer list template: plan small gifts for everyone without overspending

Short answer: Give each stocking a small budget, then list every stuffer on its own row with who it is for, the item, the price and whether it is bought. A SUMIFS shows the total per stocking, and a count of bought items per person shows which stockings still look empty.

Why stuffers need their own list

Stocking stuffers are cheap one by one, which is exactly why they blow budgets. Ten small items at a few dollars each can cost as much as a main gift. A separate list with its own budget keeps them visible.

Columns to use

Two small tables are enough.

  • Stockings: A Name, B Budget, C Spent, D Items bought, E Status
  • Stuffers: A For (who), B Item, C Price, D Bought (check box)

Formulas (Google Sheets and Excel)

  • Spent: =SUMIFS(Stuffers!C:C,Stuffers!A:A,A2,Stuffers!D:D,TRUE)
  • Items bought: =COUNTIFS(Stuffers!A:A,A2,Stuffers!D:D,TRUE)
  • Status: =IF(D2<3,"Needs more",IF(C2>B2,"Over budget","Done"))
  • Total stuffer spend: =SUM(C:C)

Ideas that fit most stockings

Mix one useful thing, one treat and one small fun item per person: socks, lip balm, a favourite snack, batteries for a gift that needs them, a puzzle book, a small toy or a gift card. Buying a few of the same item for several people can save money.

Keep it with the main gift list

Stuffers work best on the same sheet as your main gifts so one total shows the whole season. Add a Stocking column to your gift list, or keep the stuffer table on a tab next to it.

FAQ

How much should I spend on a stocking?

Whatever fits your overall budget. Many people set a small fixed amount per stocking and stick to it for everyone, which also keeps things fair between kids.

How many items go in a stocking?

Three to six small items fills most stockings. The status formula above flags any stocking with fewer than three.
