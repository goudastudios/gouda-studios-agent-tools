Holiday budget planner template: how to plan Christmas spending and stick to it

Short answer: List every holiday spending category (gifts, food, decorations, travel, cards and postage, parties, charity), give each a planned amount that adds up to a total you can afford, then log every purchase with a category. SUMIFS turns the log into an actual column, and actual minus planned shows where you are over.

Categories to include

People usually budget for gifts and forget the rest. Include all of these, even if some are small:

  • Gifts (rolled up from a per-person list)
  • Food and drink, including Christmas dinner and parties you host
  • Decorations and the tree
  • Travel and gas
  • Cards, wrapping paper and postage
  • Clothes for events, teacher and coworker gifts, tips
  • Charity and donations

Planned vs actual table

Make a Budget tab with Category in A, Planned in B, Actual in C and Difference in D, and an Expense Log tab with Date in A, Item in B, Category in C and Amount in D.

  • Actual: =SUMIFS('Expense Log'!D:D,'Expense Log'!C:C,A2)
  • Difference: =B2-C2
  • Total planned: =SUM(B:B)
  • Total actual: =SUM(C:C)

Flag overspending

Add a conditional format with the custom formula =$C2>$B2 and a red fill on the budget rows. Make the Category column on the log a dropdown that pulls from the budget categories so every purchase lands in the right row.

Pick the total first

Decide the total you can afford from savings or this month's spare cash before you split it into categories. Splitting first and adding up later is how budgets drift. If you pay with a card, include the amount in your plan only if you can pay it off in full when the statement arrives.

A five minute weekly check

Once a week from November, add any purchases to the log and glance at the Difference column. Moving money between categories is fine as long as the total stays put.
