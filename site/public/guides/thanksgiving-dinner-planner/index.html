Thanksgiving dinner planner: build a cooking timeline so turkey and sides finish together

Short answer: Pick your serving time first, then work backwards. List every dish with cook time, rest time and oven temperature, subtract those from the serving time to get each start time, and sort the list. Guest list and dietary notes

Write every guest with any allergies or diets next to their name, and what they are bringing if it is a shared meal. The guest count sets portion sizes; the dietary notes stop you from serving a gravy or stuffing nobody can eat.

  • Name
  • Adult or kid (for portion sizing)
  • Dietary notes (Vegetarian, Vegan, Gluten-free, Nut allergy, Dairy-free, No pork)
  • Bringing? (dish name or blank if you are cooking for them)

Build the Thanksgiving menu

Keep the classic core, then add only as many sides as your oven and counter space can handle.

  • Main: roast turkey (or a second main for guests who do not eat turkey)
  • Sides: mashed potatoes, stuffing or dressing, gravy, cranberry sauce, roasted vegetables or green beans, rolls
  • Dessert: pumpkin pie and one backup
  • Drinks: water, coffee, and whatever else your guests expect

One shopping list, sorted by section

Copy every ingredient from every recipe onto one list, then group it the way the store is laid out.

  • Produce: potatoes, onions, celery, carrots, herbs, citrus, salad greens
  • Meat: turkey, bacon or sausage for stuffing if you use it
  • Dairy and eggs: butter, milk, cream, eggs, cheese
  • Bakery: rolls, pie crusts if not homemade
  • Pantry: stock, flour, cranberry sauce, spices, oil, foil, roasting bag
  • Frozen: ice, backup vegetables or dessert
  • Drinks: wine, juice, sparkling water, coffee

Turkey thaw timeline (fridge)

The USDA guideline is about 24 hours of fridge thawing for every 4 to 5 pounds of turkey. Keep it in its wrapping on a tray on the bottom shelf so juices cannot drip onto other food. Example: a 16 pound bird needs about 4 days; a 20 pound bird needs about 5 days. If the bird is still icy the morning of, cold-water thawing in the sink (water changed every 30 minutes) is the backup — never thaw on the counter.

Build the cooking timeline backwards

For each dish, list cook minutes, rest minutes and oven temperature. Put the serving time in cell B1. Start time for a dish on row 3 with cook minutes in C and rest minutes in D is:

  • =$B$1-(C3+D3)/1440
  • Sort the whole list by start time so the day reads top to bottom
  • Park the turkey start time first; sides usually fit into the roast rest window

Fix oven clashes before Thursday

Write each dish's oven temperature next to its start and end. If two dishes need different temperatures in the same window, move one to the hob, slow cooker or microwave, cook it earlier and reheat it, or use the turkey rest (often 20 to 40 minutes) to blast vegetables and heat sides at a higher temp.

Day-before prep

Wednesday night is when Thanksgiving gets easy: thaw check, chop vegetables, make cranberry sauce and desserts, set the table, clear fridge space for the bird and for leftovers, and write the final timeline on one page next to the oven.

Food safety on the day

Use a meat thermometer, not the pop-up timer alone. The USDA says turkey is safe at an internal temperature of 165 degrees F in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost thigh and the innermost wing. Stuffing cooked inside the bird should also reach 165 F. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

FAQ

How do I make a Thanksgiving cooking timeline?

Pick the serving time, list every dish with cook and rest minutes, subtract those from the serving time to get each start time, then sort by start time. The spreadsheet formula =$B$1-(C3+D3)/1440 does the subtraction when B1 is the serving time.

How long does a turkey take to thaw in the fridge?

About 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds, per USDA guidance. Start several days ahead for a large bird.

What internal temperature is turkey done?

165 degrees F in the thickest breast, innermost thigh and innermost wing. Check with a thermometer rather than guessing from color or juices.
