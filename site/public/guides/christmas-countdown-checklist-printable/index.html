Christmas countdown checklist: an 8 week plan to get ready for Christmas without the last minute rush

Short answer: Work backwards from December 25 in eight weekly blocks. Do the planning jobs first (budget, gift list, card list, dates), the ordering and booking jobs next (gifts, cards, travel, the bird), and leave only wrapping, fresh food and cooking for the final week.

Week 8 (around October 30): set the plan

Decide the total you can spend, list everyone you are buying for with a budget each, and put the key dates on a calendar: parties, school events, travel days and the last shipping dates for the carriers you use.

Week 7 (around November 6): lists and early orders

Write the Christmas card list and check addresses. Start the gift list with ideas next to each name, and order anything personalised or handmade now because those take the longest.

Week 6 (around November 13): book and order

Book travel and any time off work. If you are hosting, decide the guest count and order the turkey or roast from the butcher. Order or buy Christmas cards and stamps.

Week 5 (around November 20): get ready for the sales

Go through the gift list and mark the items worth waiting for a sale on. Buy anything that is not likely to be discounted, and log each purchase as you go.

Week 4 (around November 27): Black Friday, cards and decorations

Black Friday is November 27, 2026 and Cyber Monday is November 30. Shop from your list and budget so the deals serve the plan. Then get the decorations up and write and post cards.

Week 3 (around December 4): finish shopping

Aim to have every gift bought or ordered by now so online orders arrive in time. Plan the Christmas dinner menu and write one shopping list for it, sorted by store section.

Week 2 (around December 11): wrap and ship

Wrap gifts as they arrive, post anything that has to travel, and buy the non-perishable food and drinks for Christmas.

Week 1 (December 18 to 25): fresh food and the big day

Buy fresh produce two or three days before, thaw the bird in the fridge in good time, do prep on Christmas Eve, and follow a cooking timeline on the day.

Track it in a spreadsheet if you prefer

If you would rather tick things off on your phone, put the tasks in column A, the target week in B and a checkbox in C. A progress figure keeps you honest:

  • Tasks done: =COUNTIF(C:C,TRUE)
  • Share done: =COUNTIF(C:C,TRUE)/COUNTA(A2:A)
  • Days until Christmas: =DATE(2026,12,25)-TODAY()

FAQ

When should I start planning for Christmas?

About eight weeks ahead, at the end of October, gives enough time to spread the spending and the jobs out. Starting later still works; just merge the early weeks.

What should I do first?
