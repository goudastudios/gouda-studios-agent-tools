Why track annual fees

Fees post on each card's own anniversary, so with several cards they arrive at random times through the year. Most issuers refund a fee if you close the card within a short window after it posts, often around 30 days, so knowing the date in advance is what gives you a real choice. Check your card's terms for the exact window.

Columns

One row per card:

  • Card
  • Annual fee ($)
  • Account open date
  • Next fee date
  • Days until fee
  • Value used in the last 12 months ($)
  • Net value = Value used minus Fee
  • Decision: Keep, Ask for retention offer, Downgrade, Cancel

Formulas (Google Sheets and Excel)

  • Next fee date (open date in C2): =EDATE(C2,12*(DATEDIF(C2,TODAY(),"y")+1))
  • Days until fee: =D2-TODAY()
  • Net value: =F2-B2
  • Suggested decision: =IF(G2>=0,"Keep",IF(G2>-B2/2,"Ask for retention offer","Downgrade or cancel"))
  • Total fees this year: =SUM(B2:B)
  • Fees due in the next 60 days: =SUMIFS(B:B,E:E,"<=60",E:E,">=0")

Warnings that work

Add a conditional formatting rule on the row: =AND($E2>=0,$E2<=30) in amber. Sort the sheet by Days until fee so the next decision is always at the top.

Count value honestly

Only count credits and perks you would have paid for anyway, and for rewards count the extra you earned over a no-fee card. A card that looks worth it on paper often is not once you count only what you used.

FAQ

When is a credit card annual fee charged?

Usually on the first statement after the account anniversary, the month you opened the card. Some cards charge the first fee on the first statement.

Can I get an annual fee refunded?

Many issuers refund the fee if you close or downgrade within a window after it posts. Check your card's terms before you rely on it.
