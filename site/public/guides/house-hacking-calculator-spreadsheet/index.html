House hacking calculator spreadsheet: what you pay to live in a duplex and what it earns after you move out

Short answer: Add the mortgage payment, property tax, insurance and any mortgage insurance to get the full monthly cost. Subtract the rent from the units you do not live in to get what you actually pay to live there, and compare it with renting a similar place. Then rerun it with every unit rented to see whether it cash flows as a pure rental later.

Inputs

  • Purchase price, down payment % and closing costs
  • Interest rate, loan term and monthly mortgage insurance if your loan has it
  • Property tax and insurance per year
  • Rent for each unit, vacancy % and maintenance %
  • What you would pay in rent to live somewhere similar

Core formulas (Google Sheets and Excel)

  • Loan = Price x (1 minus Down payment %)
  • Monthly mortgage: =PMT(Rate/12, Years*12, -Loan)
  • Full monthly cost = Mortgage + Tax/12 + Insurance/12 + Mortgage insurance
  • Your net cost while living there = Full monthly cost minus Rent from other units
  • Savings vs renting = Rent you would pay minus Your net cost
  • Cash flow after moving out = Rent all units x 12 x (1 minus Vacancy) minus Tax, Insurance, Mortgage insurance and Maintenance minus 12 x Mortgage

Worked example

A $400,000 duplex with 3.5% down ($14,000), a $386,000 loan at 6.75% over 30 years, $5,000 tax, $1,800 insurance and $180 a month mortgage insurance. The other unit rents for $1,800, and both units would rent for $3,600 together.

  • Mortgage: =PMT(0.0675/12,360,-386000) = $2,503.59 a month
  • Full monthly cost: $2,503.59 + $416.67 tax + $150 insurance + $180 = $3,250.26
  • Your net cost while living there: $3,250.26 minus $1,800 = about $1,450 a month
  • After moving out: $43,200 rent minus 5% vacancy = $41,040
  • Expenses: $2,160 maintenance (5%) + $5,000 tax + $1,800 insurance + $2,160 mortgage insurance = $11,120
  • Cash flow: $41,040 minus $11,120 minus $30,043 mortgage = about minus $123 a year

What the example tells you

While you live there, the house hack cuts your housing cost to about $1,450 a month, which is the real win if a similar place would cost more. As a pure rental it roughly breaks even, so the long-term return depends on loan paydown, rent growth and appreciation rather than cash flow.

FAQ

Can I use a low down payment loan for a house hack?

Owner-occupied loans for 1 to 4 unit properties often allow a smaller down payment than investment loans, with an occupancy requirement. Check the current rules with a lender.

Should I count my own unit's rent?

Not while you live there. Count it only in the move-out scenario.

Is there a template?

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an estimating method. Check numbers with a lender, accountant or local agent before buying.
